First project of my 60 solutions in 30 days challenge: MenuFlow, a digital restaurant menu that assembles the order and hands it to WhatsApp complete.

THE PROBLEM

A neighbourhood restaurant that wants to sell online usually picks between two bad options.

A delivery marketplace charges a commission on every order. On food margins that commission is often the difference between a dish being profitable and not, and the restaurant does not end up owning the customer relationship it is paying for.

A PDF menu and a phone number costs nothing and produces orders like "the usual, no onion, and one of those pastas". Reconstructing that takes several messages, during service, and it is where the wrong order comes from.

THE SOLUTION

The customer builds the order on their own phone, with the real prices and the real options, and the restaurant receives it written out in full on the channel it already uses: every line, every option, every note, unit prices and the total.

No commission per order, no payment gateway, no backend.

HOW IT IS BUILT

Money is an integer number of cents, end to end. No float ever holds a price.

A cart line is identified by the product plus its chosen options, sorted. A steak ordered rare and a steak ordered well done are two lines, not a quantity of two.

The cart is re-priced against the current catalog on every read, never trusted from storage.

No business rule lives in a component: pricing, line identity, search and message building are pure functions.

THE BUG THAT TAUGHT THE MOST

A required option group with a maximum of two opened empty. TypeScript was happy, the unit tests were green, and the dish simply refused to go into the cart without saying why. Only clicking through it in a browser found it.

It is the best argument this project gave me for testing the interface and not only the functions underneath it.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO

Opening WhatsApp is not a confirmed order. The order is confirmed when the restaurant replies, and the interface says so. Nothing simulates a payment. No delivery fee is invented: the menu carries a note saying the fee depends on the address and is settled in conversation, and that note travels with the message.

VERIFIED

82 tests passing, TypeScript strict and ESLint clean, build clean, zero axe-core violations (WCAG 2.1 A + AA) at 360, 390, 430, 768 and 1440px locally, and against the live deployment.

Not verified: physical devices, Safari and Firefox.

The restaurant, "Mesa Nove", is fictional and the WhatsApp number is a placeholder. There are no users, no clients and no revenue, so no figure of that kind appears here.

Stack: Next.js App Router, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS v4, Vitest, Testing Library.
