# Suggested visual identity for the posts

The point of reference is the application itself, not a design trend. Anyone
who clicks through from the post should recognise the thing they arrive at.

## Where the palette comes from

Read it from `src/app/globals.css` rather than from this file, which can
drift. The direction: warm neutral ground, one warm accent, dark legible
text — a premium but accessible restaurant, not a SaaS dashboard.

Use **one accent across the whole set**. If slide 2 is warm, slide 5 is not
suddenly blue.

## Typography

One family for the whole set, two weights. The headline weight carries the
hierarchy, not a second typeface. If a word needs emphasis, use the bold or
italic of the same family — never a second font.

Numbers in a mono face when they are prices, so columns line up.

## Composition

- Asymmetric, with real breathing room. Not everything centred.
- A device mockup is allowed for the mobile screenshots, as long as it does
  not crop or distort what the screen shows.
- Maximum one idea per slide.

## Explicitly avoid

- Purple-to-blue gradients, neon glow, dark mesh backgrounds. None of it is
  in the application and all of it reads as generic AI output.
- Decorative cards around content that is already legible.
- Stock photography of people in a restaurant. The food photography in the
  app is licensed from Pexels and credited; nothing else is needed.
- Fake badges, fake ratings, fake client logos. There are no clients.

## Image rules

- Preserve the content of every real screenshot exactly. Do not change text,
  numbers, features, buttons or any information from the application.
- If the interface cannot be preserved faithfully inside a composition, keep
  the original capture intact and build the composition around it.
- Never invent a screen or a client logo.
- 4:5 for the carousel, at a resolution LinkedIn will not soften.
